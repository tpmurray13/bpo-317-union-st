Zoning & nonconforming-use analysis

Per the Fayetteville UDO Table 30-4.A.2 use table for the DT-1/DT-2 downtown districts:

  • Automobile repair and servicing (without painting/body-work) is a special-use (S) in DT-1, but prohibited (/) in DT-2 — the subject's own zoning district. The building's historical auto-repair use is therefore a legally nonconforming use: it cannot be re-established without a variance or rezoning, which meaningfully narrows the pool of buyers/tenants who could resume that exact use.
  • Commercial recreation, indoor is a permitted (P) use in DT2 — meaning the current wrestling-school/sports-training occupancy is a fully conforming use, unlike the former auto-repair operation. This is a genuine positive for marketability: it demonstrates a proven, by-right reuse path and broadens the buyer/tenant pool toward general retail, flex, indoor-recreation, and office uses.
  • Other relevant DT2 findings: general "repair establishment" (non-automotive) = permitted; automotive painting/body shop = special-use; automobile parts/installation = special-use; tire/muffler sales and mounting = permitted; athletic field/clubhouse = permitted. A prospective buyer or tenant proposing any automotive-adjacent use should confirm current status directly with the City of Fayetteville Planning & Zoning Division ((910) 433-1612) before relying on this ordinance-text analysis alone.

Methodology and verification basis

This BPO values the subject as vacant/owner-occupied real estate using the sales comparison approach for the sale opinion, and a market-rent survey for the lease recommendation, cross-checked against (but not reliant upon) the county's cost-approach assessed value.

  • All comps were independently researched — no client documents were supplied for this assignment, and none of the comps below are taken from any marketing brochure; each carries its own public-record or brokerage-listing source link.
  • Three of the five sale comps are county-verified, closed-sale transactions from Cumberland County's own "Subject Area Improved Sales" report for the subject's market area (VCS 8114), the strongest available evidence. Two additional same-city sales were located and independently verified through commercial listing sources to broaden the size range represented in the grid.
  • Adjustments reflect Grant-Murray Real Estate's professional judgment as the prospective listing broker; this is a broker's opinion of value for marketing purposes, not a certified USPAP appraisal.

Grant-Murray recommends positioning 317 Union St as small downtown flex/service-commercial space rather than premium finished storefront retail, given its age and county-documented depreciation. A triple-net (NNN) structure at $9.50/SF/Yr (tenant responsible for its pro-rata share of CAM, property tax, and insurance, plus utilities) sits above the citywide flex average to capture the downtown-proximity premium, while remaining well below finished downtown retail rates the building's condition cannot support. An initial 3–5 year term with a renewal option is recommended, with the building offered largely as-is given its depreciation profile — limited landlord TI contribution should be expected. Ideal tenant profile: indoor recreation/sports training (the current, zoning-conforming use), light retail, service (non-automotive), storage/flex, or professional office. Automobile repair or servicing cannot be offered to a prospective tenant as a permitted use under current DT2 zoning — see the zoning analysis above.
